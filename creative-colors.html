Website Design

My palettes began long before this website.

For as long as I can remember, different subjects have had different colors in my mind: math feels blue, science green, and reading or writing red-orange. It isn't a rule I consciously follow. A color simply comes to mind when I think about each subject.

That association became the starting point for this portfolio. Rather than presenting one fixed identity, My Palettes gives different interests their own colors and lets them overlap — much like watercolor on paper.

Why muted colors?

I naturally gravitate toward light, nude, and low-saturation shades rather than deep or neon colors. My personal muted-autumn color analysis also helped me recognize why warm, softened tones appeal to me.

I chose a rice-paper background and gentle watercolor washes to make the site feel open, calm, and personal.

Color psychology offers possible associations — not fixed emotional effects. People respond differently depending on experience, culture, context, and preference.

These colors represent my design intentions, not promises about how every visitor will feel.

Behind the design.

The continuous New York City skyline on the homepage reflects a city full of overlapping stories. Its buildings invite you to discover different parts of me as you move across the scene.

On the inside pages, watercolor washes blend at the edges because my interests don't fit into rigid categories.

The asymmetrical editorial layouts reflect my love of magazine design, while the quieter backgrounds leave space for the work itself.

The site is also a learning project. As I develop my HTML, CSS, and JavaScript skills, its interactions and layouts will continue to change.
